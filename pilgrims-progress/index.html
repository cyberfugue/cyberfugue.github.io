The road, as you read it

For three hundred years The Pilgrim’s Progress has been read as a journey: the Slough of Despond, the Wicket Gate, the Cross, Vanity Fair, Doubting Castle, the River. This edition lays that whole road out as one long theatre of cut paper, and ties every passage of both parts to its place on it.

As you read, the pilgrims walk on. When they stop to talk, they stand. When the text brings someone on, they come up onto the stage.

I

The burden falls at the Cross

Christian carries his burden until the text reaches the Cross. Then it loosens, tumbles down the slope and falls into the sepulchre. His rags become a coat, he is given his roll, he is armed at the Palace Beautiful.

Frederick Barnard’s engravings of 1890 sit beside the passages they show, and open full size.

II

Both parts, read aloud

Two complete LibriVox readings, by Joy Chan and by MaryAnn Spiegel, about 23 hours in all, built into the app. The text follows the voice paragraph by paragraph, and the pilgrims walk with it. It plays without a connection, keeps playing with your screen locked, and has a sleep timer.

III

The Valley of the Shadow of Death

Each place has its own light and weather, sound and music: rain on the Slough, the festive dusk of the Fair, the storm over By-path Meadow, the gold of the City. In the Valley of the Shadow the stage goes dark but for a lantern about the pilgrim, and the page goes dark too, until the day breaks.

IV

Walk the Way

Step out of the book and walk the road yourself. The cast, the light and the sound are what the story has at each place. Glowing sights open notes and Bunyan’s own words, and you can read on from wherever you stand.

V

The map of the Way

A parchment chart of the road, for Christian and for Christiana, with every station drawn in the theatre’s own scenery. The way you have read is gilded; the rest lies under mist.

VI

Choose the Way

At Bunyan’s turnings, from Worldly Wiseman’s advice to the River, you choose. Each wrong turn ends as Bunyan says such turns end, in his own words: 39 passages and 15 endings.

About this edition

The text is the 1853 Derby & Miller edition of both parts, in the public domain, as are Barnard’s engravings and the LibriVox recordings. The theatre, its players and scenery, the sound and music, the map and Choose the Way were made for this edition.

The app collects no data and needs no connection. It works on iPhone and iPad with iOS 15 or later, and on iPhone Duo it resizes as you fold and unfold, keeping your place.
