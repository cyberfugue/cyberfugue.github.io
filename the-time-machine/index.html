A voyage to make yourself

From a Richmond laboratory to the world of the Eloi in the year 802,701, down into the Morlocks' Underworld, and on to the last beach under a dying sun. Every place is drawn live around you, every sound is made live, and everything set in the old face is Wells's, word for word.

“I was in my laboratory at four o'clock, and since then … I've lived eight days … such days as no human being ever lived before!”
I

Press the lever over, and go

The laboratory goes “faint and hazy, then fainter and ever fainter”. Drag the starting lever and the dials run; the stopping lever brakes, and past rest takes you back. Bring the machine down on a date, or come in too hot and be flung headlong.

II

Hours a second to ten million years

Night follows day “like the flapping of a black wing”, the sun becomes “a brilliant arch”, and huge buildings rise up faint and fair. In Take the machine there is no story, just the machine and the world at every year: set a dial-stop for the Golden Age, the beach of the crabs or the end of the world, or simply go.

III

Take the pen

The whole story in five acts, in Wells's own words throughout: the model on the octagonal table, the White Sphinx in the hail, the little people of the Golden Age, and the machine gone at moonrise.

IV

Weena

Down the well into the Underworld, the Palace of Green Porcelain with its matches and camphor and iron bar, and Weena at your side, until the night the forest burns.

V

Fire against the dark

The forest on fire, the trap of the White Sphinx, the last beach and the eclipse, and home with two white flowers in your pocket, if you have earned them.

VI

The whole book, read aloud

The complete 1895 text, with the world behind the page at the place and the hour of each passage. Listen to Cliff Stone's LibriVox reading, word-timed so the page follows the voice. It is inside the app and plays without a connection.

About this edition

The text is the 1895 Heinemann edition of The Time Machine, in the public domain. The narration is the LibriVox recording read by Cliff Stone, also in the public domain. The realistic people are from Microsoft's Rocketbox library. Everything else seen and heard was made for this edition: four ages of the world, about an hour and a half.

The app collects no data. It works on iPhone and iPad with iOS 15 or later; headphones are best.
