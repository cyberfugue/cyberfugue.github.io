A little window upon a great world

On the morning after his twenty-first birthday, Anodos wakes to find the stream from his washing-basin running across his carpet, and his room turning into a wood. He follows it into Fairy Land: to the Ash and the Beech, a lady sleeping in alabaster, a palace of white marble, a Shadow that will not leave him, and a cottage of four doors by the sea. George MacDonald’s first prose romance was the book that, as C. S. Lewis said, baptised his imagination.

“In good sooth, my masters, this is no door. Yet is it a little window, that looketh upon a great world.”
I

The plates come alive

John Bell’s twenty-five plates for the illustrated edition of 1894 sit beside the passages they show. When one reaches the middle of the screen it begins, very quietly, to live: the sea moves, the moon breathes, a fire flickers, mist drifts, specks of light go through the air. A plate seen for the first time develops out of the paper, as a print does.

II

Read aloud, word by word

Both LibriVox readings are inside the app: Brad Powers and Thomas A. Copeland. Every word of the book is timed to each, so the word being spoken is lit, the page follows the voice, and Fairy Land and its sound follow it too. Change readers at the same place; tap any paragraph to hear it from there. It plays with the screen locked.

III

Sing her out of the stone

Where the book gives Anodos something to do, you can do it: open the little drawers of his father’s old secretary; play a harp to sing the marble lady out of her alabaster, the stone clearing from her face as the song goes on; touch the maiden’s crystal globe, gently, three times; sing in the hall of Phantasy; choose among the old woman’s four doors.

IV

A Shadow that follows the page

In the eighth chapter Anodos opens a closet he was told not to, and finds his Shadow. From then on it follows the page as it follows him, and where it falls the plates turn grey and the painted world drains of its colour, as the fairy child in the book turned commonplace when he stood in it.

V

A painted, sounding Fairy Land

Behind the text the wood is painted live: Anodos’s room becoming the forest, the fairies’ flowers lit at night, the storm over the Beech, the white palace by the river, the hall of statues, the wintry sea and the island cottage. Step out to look around, and the place answers your touch. Every sound and the music, in the mode of each place, is made as you read.

VI

A chart of Fairy Land

MacDonald drew no map; this one is made from the book, as an engraved chart: from Anodos’s house through the wood, by the river to the palace, under the earth, out to the sea and the island, and home again. The way read so far is gilded; the rest lies under mist.

About this edition

Phantastes: A Faerie Romance for Men and Women was first published in 1858. The text is the 1905 edition, edited by the author’s son Greville MacDonald, in the public domain. John Bell’s plates come from Chatto & Windus’s edition of 1894; Greville MacDonald so disliked them that he bought up that edition and had it pulped, but some copies survived. The readings by Brad Powers and Thomas A. Copeland are LibriVox’s, in the public domain. The painted Fairy Land, the life of the plates, the sound and music, the chart and the moments were made for this edition.

Everything is inside the app and works with no connection. The app collects no data. Headphones are best.
