The War of the Worlds: help

Questions or problems: write to cyberfugue@gmail.com.

Say which device you’re using and what happened, and you’ll get a reply as soon as possible.

What’s free, and what the purchase opens

Chapter I of Book One, The Eve of the War, is free, with everything the edition does: the war drawn beside the text, both readings word by word, watch, the sound and music, the map, Ogilvy’s telescope, the Martian preserved in spirits (from the title page), and all of Alvim Corrêa’s plates to look through. One in-app purchase, The whole book ($3.99 in the US; the title page shows your own store’s price), opens the rest: the other twenty-six chapters of both Books, both readings of all of them, every scene drawn as it is read, the whole map and its days, and the other moments (the lid of the cylinder, the slit at Sheen, the coal cellar, following the “Ulla”). There is no subscription and there are no ads.

Restoring your purchase

On a new phone or tablet, or after reinstalling, open the app and tap Restore purchase. It’s on the purchase’s screen, which appears where the free part ends (at the end of Chapter I) or when you tap Buy the whole book on the title page, and under The whole book in Settings. Use the same Apple Account (iPhone and iPad) or Google account (Android) you bought with. You won’t be charged again.

Refunds

Purchases are handled by Apple or Google. On iPhone and iPad, ask for a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com, signed in with your Apple Account. On Android, go to your Google Play order history and choose the purchase.

The readers

Tap listen in the bar, or Begin, read aloud on the title page. Two LibriVox readings are inside the app, so they play without a connection, with the silent switch on and with the screen locked: johnnyenglish and Cori Samuel. Choose between them under Settings → The reader’s voice; the reading carries on from the same place. The word being read is lit; turn that off with Light each word as it is read. Tap any paragraph to hear it from there. The dock above the bar goes back and on fifteen seconds, changes the speed, sets a sleep timer, and lets the page follow the voice or not; the lock screen and headphones can pause and play. watch in the bar shows the drawing full size with the words beneath, read aloud.

Sound

The sound of the war, and its music, are made as you read. They are on until you turn them off: check sound in the bar (it reads “sound on” or “sound off”), and the levels of the places and the music in Settings. Headphones are best.

The drawn war

The war is drawn live beside the text, and what the text tells happens in it as you read. Drag it to look about. If your device runs warm, or you would rather read plain text, turn it off under Settings → The drawn world; there too you can have it drawn plainly instead of in pen and ink, and stop the page darkening with the hour.

On iPhone Duo

Opened, the phone sets the reading as a newspaper’s front page, with the war drawn as the tall engraving beside the text. Opening it while you read spreads the war across the whole page for a moment; opening it while the book is read aloud shows the war full size with the words beneath (watch); folding it again returns to the page at the same word.

Your place

The app remembers your place and your settings on that device. Continue on the title page takes you back to it; Begin always starts at the beginning of the book; home in the bar returns to the title page from anywhere. Your place doesn’t sync between devices. To start afresh, tap Reset progress under Your progress in Settings; to keep nothing between visits, turn off Keep my place on this device there. Your settings stay.

Requirements

iPhone or iPad with a recent iOS, or an Android phone or tablet with Android 7 or later. The war is drawn live, so a recent device gives the smoothest picture.

Privacy

The app collects no data. There are no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. The full privacy policy has the details.

The War of the Worlds, by H. G. Wells (1898), is in the public domain, as are Henrique Alvim Corrêa’s plates of 1906 and the LibriVox readings by johnnyenglish and Cori Samuel. The drawn war, the sound and music, the map and the moments were made for this edition.
