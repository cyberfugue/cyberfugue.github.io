Other Worlds · iPhone, iPad and Android

Other Worlds: help

Questions or problems: write to cyberfugue@gmail.com.

Say which device you’re using, which book, and what happened, and you’ll get a reply as soon as possible.

The shelf

Other Worlds opens on a shelf of seven books: Flatland, Phantastes, The Pilgrim’s Progress, The Night Land, The Time Machine, The War of the Worlds and The First Men in the Moon. Tap a book to open it at its own title page. From there, Back to the library returns to the shelf; inside a book, home in the bar returns to that book’s title page. On Android, the back gesture closes whatever is open in the book, then goes back to the shelf.

What’s free, and what the purchases open

The first chapter of every book is free, with what comes before it and with everything its edition does: Flatland’s first section, the mottoes and Chapter I of Phantastes, the prologue and Chapter I of The Night Land, Chapter I of The Time Machine, of The War of the Worlds and of The First Men in the Moon. The Pilgrim’s Progress is free, all of it.

Two kinds of in-app purchase open the rest, once, with no subscription and no ads:

  • The whole book ($3.99 in the US), one for each of the six other books, opens that book.
  • All the books ($17.99 in the US) opens all six, and every book added to Other Worlds later, at no further cost.

The shelf shows your own store’s price under each book. Tap Buy the whole book under a book, or Buy all the books beneath the shelf; inside a book, the offer appears where its free part ends. If you’ve bought a book on its own, you can still buy all the books later for the others.

Restoring your purchases

On a new phone or tablet, or after reinstalling, open the app, tap settings in the shelf’s bar and then Restore purchases. Each book’s offer has Restore purchase too. Use the same Apple Account (iPhone and iPad) or Google account (Android) you bought with. You won’t be charged again.

Refunds

Purchases are handled by Apple or Google. On iPhone and iPad, ask for a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com, signed in with your Apple Account. On Android, go to your Google Play order history and choose the purchase.

The readings

To keep the app small, each book’s reading is downloaded the first time you listen to it, from the App Store or Google Play, from about 100 MB to about 260 MB a book. A notice at the top of the screen shows how far it has come; you can read the text meanwhile, and the voice begins as soon as it’s there. From then on the reading plays without a connection. A Wi-Fi connection is best for the first listen; on Android, Google Play may ask before downloading over mobile data.

If a download stops, tap the notice to try again. To free the space, open settings on the shelf: Readings on this device lists each book’s reading and its size, with Remove. A removed reading is downloaded again the next time you listen.

The First Men in the Moon

H. G. Wells’s novel of 1901, complete, read aloud by Cliff Stone for LibriVox, each word lit as it is spoken, with Claude Shepperson’s plates from the first edition. It was made for Other Worlds and can be read in three dresses. Choose one on the book’s title page, under Read it as, or in its Settings, under The dress; change at any time and the book opens again at the same place, the reading at the same moment. The text, the reading and your place are the same in all three; what is drawn beside the text, the sound and music, and the places to explore are each dress’s own.

  • Through the Glass: the voyage seen through windows, round, in a brass frame (Bedford’s window at Lympne, the sphere’s glass with its Cavorite blinds, the Moon through the glass and through the Selenites’ grating). Its places, on the bar: steer, the sphere to work by its twelve blinds and fall home as Bedford did (once you have read to Chapter XX), and inside, the sphere’s round room, each thing in it with the words that tell it. On iPhone Duo, opened, you read inside the sphere; open the phone and the blinds shut, and the words float loose before they settle.
  • The Lunar Day: the world painted at Bedford’s eye-height, the crater kept to the sun’s own time, from the dawn to the coming night. Its places: crater, the search for the sphere (touch and hold to gather, let go to leap; find the sphere before the cliff’s shadow reaches you; once you have read to Chapter XVIII), and dawn, the lunar dawn to watch. On iPhone Duo, opened, the crater stands from rim to rim with the sun’s arc; open the phone and you make Bedford’s first slow leap.
  • The Moon Within: Cavor’s cut-away, in blue on cream, each part drawn in when the text first tells of it, with “Drawn in” beside the paragraph. Its places: cut-away, the drawings full size (drag, pinch or the magnifiers; tap a part for the words that told it), and messages, Cavor’s signals as Mr. Wendigee received them, from Chapter XXII. On iPhone Duo, opened, the Moon is split open across the top; open the phone and the whole Moon cleaves in two.

Chapter I, Mr. Bedford Meets Mr. Cavor at Lympne, is free, in all three dresses. The whole book ($3.99) opens the other twenty-five chapters in all three, with each dress’s places; All the books opens it too. What a dress’s places keep (a search won, the sphere brought home) is kept with your progress, and Reset progress forgets it; the dress you chose stays chosen.

Your place

Each book keeps your place and settings on that device, separately from the others, and its Continue takes you back. Reset progress and Keep my place on this device are in each book’s own Settings, under Your progress, and touch only that book. Your place doesn’t sync between devices as you read, but it is part of your device’s backup (iCloud Backup, or Google’s backup on Android), so it comes back when you set up a new phone or tablet from that backup (and on Android when you install the app again). Your purchases come back with Restore purchase.

Age suitability

Other Worlds is rated 13+ on the App Store (about PEGI 12 or ESRB Teen on Google Play). The books are classics written for grown-up readers, told in their own old-fashioned prose and read aloud as written. Nothing is shown in blood or gore: the worlds around the text are painted, drawn in ink or made of light. The rating comes from the most frightening of them, The War of the Worlds; most of the others, on their own, would suit readers of nine and over. Book by book:

  • Flatland: a satire with violence told, not shown: the Colour Revolt put down with a massacre, A Square imprisoned.
  • Phantastes: fighting with giants, a death in the wood, and some uncanny figures (the grasping Ash, the Alder Maiden, the goblins, the Shadow).
  • The Pilgrim’s Progress: spiritual peril; Christian fights Apollyon, Faithful is put to death at Vanity Fair, giants beat the pilgrims, and the Valley of the Shadow of Death is dark.
  • The Night Land: a dying world, frequent dread (the Watchers, the Night-Hounds, the House of Silence) and fighting with monsters.
  • The Time Machine: the Morlocks in the dark, driven off with fire and an iron bar; a forest burns.
  • The War of the Worlds: the most intense: people burned by the Heat-Ray, towns on fire, a death, the dead in London’s streets, and the Martians feeding on blood.
  • The First Men in the Moon: peril on the Moon, a fight with Selenites in which Bedford kills several with a crowbar, the carcasses of a butchers’ cave, and Cavor’s fate left dark at the end.

The app has no chat, no accounts, no advertising and nothing shared with other people, and it collects no data. It doesn’t browse the web: its only links out are to these help and privacy pages and to LibriVox, where the readings come from, and they open in the phone’s own browser. Its purchases are made through the App Store or Google Play, so their family controls apply: on iPhone and iPad, Ask to Buy (Family Sharing) and Screen Time’s content and purchase restrictions; on Android, Google Family Link and Google Play’s purchase approvals.

Requirements

iPhone or iPad with iOS 15 or later, or an Android phone or tablet with Android 7 or later. Several books draw their worlds live, so a recent device gives the smoothest picture.

Privacy

The app collects no data. There are no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. The full privacy policy has the details.

The seven books, their pictures and the LibriVox readings are in the public domain. The worlds drawn around them, the sound and music, the maps and each edition’s own places were made for these editions.
